1. TENANT PROTECTION ACT — JUST CAUSE, RENT CAP, TERMINATION NOTICE
================================================================================
- Cal. Civ. Code section 1946.2 — Just cause for termination after a tenant has
  continuously and lawfully occupied a unit for 12 months. Just cause must be
  stated in the written termination notice. Waivers of just cause are void
  (section 1946.2(h)). At-fault and no-fault grounds are defined by statute.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1946.2.&lawCode=CIV

- Cal. Civ. Code section 1947.12 — Rent cap: over any 12-month period, no increase
  above the LOWER of (5% + regional CPI) or 10% of the lowest rent in the prior
  12 months; no more than two increases per 12 months within the cap.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1947.12.&lawCode=CIV

- Cal. Civ. Code section 1946.1 (and section 1946 default 30-day rule) —
  Month-to-month termination notice periods: owner must give 60 days if any tenant
  has resided one year or more, otherwise 30 days; tenant generally gives 30 days.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1946.1.&lawCode=CIV

- SB 567 (2023-2024) — Amended section 1946.2 effective April 1, 2024: added the
  90-day move-in / 12-month primary-residence conditions for owner/relative
  move-in no-fault terminations, tightened "substantially remodel"/demolition
  requirements (permit copies, 30-day-vacancy test), and enhanced remedies
  (actual damages, up to treble damages for willful conduct, attorney's fees,
  offer-back obligation).
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB567

- No-fault relocation assistance = one month's rent, paid within 15 days of the
  notice or by written waiver of the final month's rent (section 1946.2(d)).

- EXEMPTIONS (section 1946.2(e) and section 1947.12(d)) are fact-dependent:
  single-family homes/condominiums (only if the owner is NOT a REIT, corporation,
  or LLC with a corporate member, AND the required exemption notice is given);
  owner-occupied duplex; certificate of occupancy within the last 15 years
  (rolling); deed-restricted affordable; certain owner-occupied room/ADU rentals;
  and units under a more protective local ordinance.

- Sunset: the Tenant Protection Act sunsets January 1, 2030 absent renewal
  (sections 1946.2(g), 1947.12(j)). It is a FLOOR; stricter local ordinances
  control.

- EXEMPTION NOTICE (verbatim; include ONLY in the optional exemption addendum and
  ONLY after confirming the property qualifies) — sections 1946.2(e)(8)(B) and
  1947.12(d)(5)(B):
  "This property is not subject to the rent limits imposed by Section 1947.12 of
   the Civil Code and is not subject to the just cause requirements of Section
   1946.2 of the Civil Code. This property meets the requirements of Sections
   1947.12 (d)(5) and 1946.2 (e)(8) of the Civil Code and the owner is not any of
   the following: (1) a real estate investment trust, as defined by Section 856 of
   the Internal Revenue Code; (2) a corporation; or (3) a limited liability company
   in which at least one member is a corporation."
  For tenancies commenced/renewed on or after July 1, 2020, this must appear in the
  written agreement. If it is not given, the unit is NOT exempt.

DRAFTING NOTE: The lease does NOT assume any exemption and does NOT bake in any
termination reason or notice period. Section 24 references both the 30-day and
60-day thresholds and defers just-cause/relocation to the applicable law at the
time of termination.

2. SECURITY DEPOSIT
================================================================================
- Cal. Civ. Code section 1950.5 — Governs residential security deposits.
  https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1950.5.&lawCode=CIV

- AB 12 (2023, operative July 1, 2024) — Deposit cap = ONE month's rent (furnished
  or unfurnished). Small-landlord exception = up to TWO months if the landlord is a
  natural person (or an LLC of all natural persons) owning no more than two
  residential rental properties totaling no more than four units; never two months
  for an active-duty service-member tenant.

- 21-calendar-day return/itemization after the tenant surrenders possession
  (section 1950.5(g)). Deductions over $125 require attached receipts/invoices.

- AB 2801 (2024) — Photographic documentation (move-out before and after
  repairs/cleaning; move-in photos for tenancies beginning on or after July 1,
  2025) and limits on cleaning-related charges (no blanket "professional cleaning"
  charge).

- AB 414 (2025, effective January 1, 2026) — If the deposit or rent was received
  electronically, the refund is generally returned electronically, and electronic
  delivery of the itemized statement requires the tenant's express written consent
  to the specific method.

DRAFTING NOTE: The deposit AMOUNT is left blank; Section 5 references the statutory
cap and instructs the parties to confirm the applicable limit and not exceed it.

4. FEES
================================================================================
- Late fees — Cal. Civ. Code section 1671(c)-(d); Orozco v. Casimiro (2004)
  121 Cal.App.4th Supp. 7. A residential late fee is valid only as a good-faith
  pre-estimate of the landlord's actual costs; a percentage-of-rent or penalty late
  fee is an unenforceable penalty. No fixed statewide dollar cap.
- Returned-payment / NSF fee — Cal. Civ. Code section 1719(a): statutory service
  charge up to $25 for the first returned item and up to $35 for each subsequent
  item (or, alternatively, statutory damages after a proper written demand).

DRAFTING NOTE: Section 7 leaves the late fee, grace period, and returned-payment
amount blank and states the charges must be legally permissible amounts, with the
NSF charge tied to the Civil Code section 1719 maximum.

5. SELF-HELP PROHIBITION AND NON-WAIVER
================================================================================
- No self-help eviction — Cal. Civ. Code section 789.3 (no willful utility
  interruption or blocking of access to force a tenant out; liability includes
  actual damages plus up to $100/day, minimum $250, plus attorney's fees).
- No harassment / constructive eviction — Cal. Civ. Code section 1940.2 (no force,
  threats, or menacing conduct; civil penalty up to $2,000 per violation).
- Recovery of possession requires the unlawful detainer process (Code of Civil
  Procedure section 1159 et seq.), a judgment, and a sheriff-performed lockout.
- Non-waivable tenant rights — Cal. Civ. Code section 1953 (clauses purporting to
  waive certain statutory rights are void).

DRAFTING NOTE: Section 23 (Breach and Remedies) and Section 24 (Termination), plus
the Owner Guidance page, expressly state the landlord will not lock out, remove
belongings, shut off utilities, or otherwise use self-help, and must use the lawful
court process. No clause grants the landlord any such power.
